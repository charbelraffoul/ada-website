# Oil Shocks, Sectors, and Portfolio Design

Oil price shocks are a recurring source of macroeconomic stress, yet their impact is far from uniform across financial markets. Some sectors are highly exposed to energy costs, while others act as defensive buffers.

To build intuition, we use a simple analogy throughout this data story: **oil behaves like a moody boss**. On calm days, markets operate normally. On bad days, sudden oil price shocks force every department of the economy to react. Different departments—representing economic sectors—respond in different ways depending on their exposure and the broader economic environment. This analogy is purely illustrative; all results are based on formal statistical and causal analysis.

In this project, we study how oil price shocks affect sector-level returns, how these effects vary across economic regimes, and what this implies for cyclical versus defensive investment strategies.

## When the boss snaps: sector responses to oil shocks

When oil prices experience a sudden shock, not all sectors react in the same way. Energy-intensive sectors face immediate cost pressures, while others are affected more indirectly through demand, inflation, or financial conditions.

Continuing the analogy, when the “boss” has a bad day, some departments absorb the shock better than others. We quantify these differences by measuring sector-level returns around identified oil shock events.
