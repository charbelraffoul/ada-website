<span class="ds-kicker">A data story</span>

  <h1 class="ds-h1">Oil shocks, sectors, and portfolio design</h1>

  <p class="ds-lead">
    Oil price shocks are a recurring source of stress for financial markets, but their impact is far from uniform.
    Some sectors react immediately through costs and margins, others through demand and risk sentiment,
    while a few appear comparatively resilient.
  </p>

  <p class="ds-lead">
    To make sense of these heterogeneous responses, imagine the following:
    <strong>oil as a boss</strong>.
    On some days, decisions are predictable and business runs smoothly.
    On other days, sudden changes force every part of the organization to react.
    Different teams are affected in different ways, depending on their role and exposure.
  </p>

  <p class="ds-lead">
    This analogy is meant to build intuition.
    The analysis itself is entirely data-driven and focuses on measuring how sector returns respond to oil shocks,
    how these responses vary across market environments, and what this implies for portfolio construction.
  </p>

  <div class="ds-divider"></div>

  <!-- CORE IDEA -->
  <div class="ds-grid">
    <div class="ds-card span-6">
      <span class="ds-tag">Core idea</span>
      <h3>One shock, many outcomes</h3>
      <p>
        We treat oil price shocks as external stress events and study how different sectors respond around them.
        The goal is not to explain markets in general, but to isolate patterns that consistently emerge
        when oil prices move abruptly.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="ds-card span-6">
      <span class="ds-tag">Why it matters</span>
      <h3>From explanation to allocation</h3>
      <p>
        If sectors react differently to the same shock, portfolio design should reflect that.
        Understanding these dynamics helps clarify the trade-off between growth-oriented and defensive strategies,
        especially during periods of stress.
      </p>
    </div>

  </div>

  <div class="ds-divider"></div>

  <!-- RESEARCH QUESTIONS -->
  <h2 style="margin: 8px 0 12px 0; letter-spacing:-0.01em;">What we study</h2>

  <div class="ds-grid">
    <div class="ds-card span-4">
      <span class="ds-tag">RQ1</span>
      <h3>Sector responses</h3>
      <p>
        How do different economic sectors behave around oil shock events?
        Which sectors appear most exposed, and which behave more defensively?
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="ds-card span-4">
      <span class="ds-tag">RQ2</span>
      <h3>Market environments</h3>
      <p>
        Do these sector responses change across different market regimes?
        Does the same shock have different effects depending on the broader environment?
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="ds-card span-4">
      <span class="ds-tag">RQ3</span>
      <h3>Portfolio implications</h3>
      <p>
        How do cyclical and defensive portfolios perform around oil shocks,
        and what does this imply for managing downside risk?
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="ds-card span-6">
      <span class="ds-tag">Causal perspective</span>
      <h3>Interpreting the effects</h3>
      <p>
        We complement the empirical analysis with a causal framework to assess whether
        observed patterns are consistent with oil shocks having a direct effect on sector returns.
      </p>
    </div>

    <div class="ds-card span-6">
      <span class="ds-tag">Next</span>
      <h3>Starting with sector exposure</h3>
      <p>

        before turning to regimes and portfolio-level consequences.
      </p>
